4 Core UX Interaction Patterns

01 · Non-Intrusive Moment Chip01:42

As the documentary plays, a synchronized bottom chip surfaces the active empire/event. Viewers can either Tap to Pause & Read immediately or tap + Save to read after the film.

02 · Docked Pip + 3-Depth DrawerAuto-Pause

Pausing or tapping a topic shrinks the video into a Sticky Top Mini-Player with a 1-tap "Resume at 01:42" bar while a 3-layer card (30s Summary / Empire Map / Archival Essay) opens below.

03 · Era Timeline Nodes & EntitiesScrubber

Milestone nodes on the timeline and underlined entities in the live transcript let curious viewers jump directly into any dynasty or monument card without losing their playback anchor.

04 · End-of-Film Reading StackBookmarks

Viewers who prefer uninterrupted cinema tap + Save during playback; saved cards stack neatly in the Saved Queue with exact video timestamp jump-backs.

Why This Solves the Viewer Dilemma

The Core UX Problem:
In traditional video pages, reading long articles forces the user to scroll away from the video or lose their timestamp. In traditional text pages, embedded videos feel disconnected from historical context.
Our Single-Spine Mobile Solution:
1. Zero-Friction Transition: One tap on the floating Moment Chip pauses playback and docks the video into a compact 158px sticky header.

2. 3 Scannable Reading Depths: Casual viewers read the 30-Sec Quick Card and tap ▶ Resume Watching in 15 seconds; history buffs switch to Dynasty Map or Archival Deep-Dive.

3. Dual Pacing (Read Now vs. Save for Later): Adding a 1-tap + Save button respects both lean-back watchers and lean-forward readers.
